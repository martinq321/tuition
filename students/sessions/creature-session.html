Make it move

A one-hour investigation with paper, patterns and a creature you can change.

Before you start

Bring scrap paper, coloured pens, scissors, removable tape, string and paper circles. Make a short chain of circles with two drawn legs per circle. Keep the screen alongside the making activity.

Goal: predict, change one thing, observe, and explain. Counting or writing is optional at entry; reasoning can be spoken or drawn.

A flexible hour

  1. 0–10 · Invent: draw a creature. Choose colours, body shape and a route.
  2. 10–20 · Build: make 6 paper segments. Attach two legs to each. Predict the effect of adding one segment.
  3. 20–35 · Test: recreate it on screen. Compare 6, 8, 10 and 12 segments. Hide the answer with your hand before predicting.
  4. 35–45 · Change: choose the wave setting or body width. Change only that control. Pause and sketch two examples.
  5. 45–55 · Investigate: try the notebook 3:2 loop. Trace it with string. Does the body follow the same route at every setting?
  6. 55–60 · Share: save the creature and finish “I thought… I changed… I noticed…”

Choose the challenge

Design first: two choices at a time, short predict–try–describe cycles, counting in pairs with physical pieces. A successful outcome can be explaining one change. Reduce demands if the learner loses interest.

Reasoning next: make 24 legs in two ways by discussing alternative legs-per-segment rules on paper. The screen uses exactly two legs per segment. Explain 2n, then work backwards from 30 legs. Test wave settings 0°, 90° and 180° and describe which segments move together.

Deeper: a route parameter does not necessarily measure distance. Compare equally spaced points along a drawn route with equally spaced times in a formula. The simulation samples by distance, approximately, to keep segment spacing steady.

Accessibility versus depth: practical entry is suitable for many primary-age learners; generalising 2n suits learners ready for multiplicative reasoning. Arc length, moving frames and sine phase are later-secondary or beyond. These are entry guides, not fixed age judgements or diagnostic assessments.
