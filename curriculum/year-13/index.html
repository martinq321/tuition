A-level Mathematics

Year 13 mathematics

The second year develops calculus, numerical solution and mathematical modelling. Compare analytic and approximate answers, evaluate statistical evidence and use vector equations and moments to test a mechanical model.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Pure

    Functions, rational expressions and proof

    Define a function with its domain and range. Find a composite and its domain, and restrict a domain when necessary to construct an inverse. Relate inverse graphs by reflection in y = x.

    View objectives (5)
    • Define a function with its domain and range. Find a composite and its domain, and restrict a domain when necessary to construct an inverse. Relate inverse graphs by reflection in y = x.
    • Sketch the modulus of a linear expression and intersections with other graphs. Apply a sequence of graph transformations, keeping track of their order.
    • Simplify and divide rational expressions while retaining excluded values. Decompose proper fractions with constant or linear numerators into at most three partial-fraction terms with linear factors in the denominator, including factors repeated twice.
    • Prove that √2 is irrational and that there are infinitely many primes by contradiction. Adapt the method to an unfamiliar claim and identify the step that creates the contradiction.
    • Compare candidate functions for a context by inspecting domain, range and end behaviour. Explain which observations would justify changing a model.
  • Pure

    Sequences, series and binomial approximations

    Generate a sequence from an explicit term or a recurrence and investigate whether it is increasing, decreasing or periodic. Translate sums into and out of sigma notation.

    View objectives (5)
    • Generate a sequence from an explicit term or a recurrence and investigate whether it is increasing, decreasing or periodic. Translate sums into and out of sigma notation.
    • Find an unknown term, common difference or finite sum for an arithmetic sequence. Explain the sum formula by pairing terms.
    • Find terms and finite sums for a geometric sequence. Determine whether an infinite geometric sum exists and use its value only when the common ratio has magnitude below one.
    • Build a sequence model for regular deposits, repayments or a repeated process. Interpret the indexing and test what happens when a parameter changes.
    • Expand a binomial with a rational exponent about zero, stating the interval of validity. Use the first few terms for an approximation and check it numerically.
  • Pure

    Radians, trigonometric functions and identities

    Convert degrees to radians and find arc lengths and sector areas. Use sin θ ≈ θ, tan θ ≈ θ and cos θ ≈ 1 − θ²/2 for small angles measured in radians.

    View objectives (5)
    • Convert degrees to radians and find arc lengths and sector areas. Use sin θ ≈ θ, tan θ ≈ θ and cos θ ≈ 1 − θ²/2 for small angles measured in radians.
    • Sketch secant, cosecant and cotangent. For integer k, exclude π/2 + kπ from the domain of secant and kπ from the domains of cosecant and cotangent. Read off |y| ≥ 1 for secant and cosecant and all real y for cotangent. Sketch arcsin, arccos and arctan on their principal branches and state their domains and ranges.
    • Use the reciprocal Pythagorean identities and the sum, difference and double-angle formulae for sine, cosine and tangent. Explain a geometric proof of the addition formulae.
    • Combine a sine and cosine of the same argument into one phase-shifted sinusoid. Find its extreme values and solve equations over a specified interval.
    • Prove trigonometric identities on their valid domains, and use trigonometry to interpret a periodic model or resolve a vector.
  • Pure

    Vectors in three dimensions

    Write spatial position and displacement vectors in component form. Find 3D distances, magnitudes, directions and unit vectors.

    View objectives (3)
    • Write spatial position and displacement vectors in component form. Find 3D distances, magnitudes, directions and unit vectors.
    • Calculate a resultant, a point dividing a segment or an unknown point from vector information, checking the result geometrically.
    • Test whether three points are collinear or two directions are parallel. Interpret a vector result in a spatial or motion context.
  • Statistics

    Conditional probability and the Normal distribution

    Use a two-way table, tree or Venn diagram to change the reference group in a conditional probability. Calculate P(A given B) from the intersection and check the condition for independence.

    View objectives (5)
    • Use a two-way table, tree or Venn diagram to change the reference group in a conditional probability. Calculate P(A given B) from the intersection and check the condition for independence.
    • Compare risk before and after new information. Explain how changing independence or an assumed base rate changes a conditional probability.
    • Relate a Normal density curve to a histogram, its centre and spread, and its two inflection points. Interpret areas as probabilities.
    • Use a calculator for Normal probabilities and inverse Normal problems. Standardise a value, or recover an unknown mean or standard deviation from given probabilities.
    • Choose between binomial and Normal models. Check when a Normal approximation to a binomial is reasonable, apply a continuity correction and compare with an exact calculation.
  • Mechanics

    Resolved forces and friction

    Choose axes along and perpendicular to a slope or another direction of motion. Resolve weight, reaction, tension and applied forces consistently.

    View objectives (4)
    • Choose axes along and perpendicular to a slope or another direction of motion. Resolve weight, reaction, tension and applied forces consistently.
    • Find equilibrium under coplanar forces or apply Newton’s second law to the components of a resultant. Use separate diagrams for connected bodies.
    • Use F ≤ μR for static friction, identify the limiting case, and determine the direction of friction from the possible motion. Use the sliding-friction model where appropriate.
    • Test a proposed state of rest, impending motion or acceleration against the equations and friction bound. Interpret a threshold angle or applied force.

Spring

  • Pure

    Parametric curves and motion

    Trace a parametric curve over a specified interval, marking the direction of travel and points reached more than once.

    View objectives (3)
    • Trace a parametric curve over a specified interval, marking the direction of travel and points reached more than once.
    • Eliminate a parameter to obtain a Cartesian relation, retaining range restrictions. Give a parametrisation for a familiar line or curve.
    • Model two coordinates using a common time parameter. Interpret intersections, allowable times and any discrepancies between the model and the observed path.
  • Pure

    Differentiation rules and the shape of a curve

    Derive the derivatives of sin x and cos x from first principles in radians. Differentiate trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic expressions, including aᵏˣ and tan kx.

    View objectives (5)
    • Derive the derivatives of sin x and cos x from first principles in radians. Differentiate trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic expressions, including aᵏˣ and tan kx.
    • Choose and combine the chain, product and quotient rules. Apply them to related rates and find the derivative of an inverse function at a suitable point.
    • Find first derivatives of implicit relations and parametric curves. Use them to locate a tangent or normal and consider where a formula for its gradient breaks down.
    • Use first and second derivatives to describe monotonicity, stationary points and curvature. Justify a point of inflection by a change of concavity.
    • Build an objective function from a practical constraint, test boundary cases and interpret the optimum with its units.
  • Pure

    Integration techniques and areas between curves

    Describe a definite integral as the limit of rectangle sums, then use the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to evaluate it.

    View objectives (5)
    • Describe a definite integral as the limit of rectangle sums, then use the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to evaluate it.
    • Integrate exponentials, sine and cosine of linear arguments and reciprocals, including the logarithmic antiderivative of 1/x on an appropriate interval.
    • Recognise a reverse chain rule or choose a one-step substitution. Transform the differential and either change the limits or return to the original variable.
    • Derive integration by parts from the product rule and apply it repeatedly where necessary. Use a partial-fraction decomposition to integrate rational expressions with linear factors.
    • Find intersections and split the interval where necessary to calculate the area enclosed between curves.
  • Pure

    Numerical roots and quadrature

    Bracket a root using continuity and a sign change. Show how a discontinuity can mislead and how an even-multiplicity root can be missed.

    View objectives (5)
    • Bracket a root using continuity and a sign change. Show how a discontinuity can mislead and how an even-multiplicity root can be missed.
    • Rearrange an equation as a fixed-point iteration, calculate its successive estimates and draw staircase or cobweb diagrams between y = x and y = g(x). Use the diagrams to explain convergence, oscillation or divergence.
    • Derive a Newton–Raphson step from a tangent, iterate from a starting value and recognise failure near a zero gradient or convergence to a different root.
    • Approximate a definite integral using the composite trapezium rule. Use the curve’s shape, rectangle bounds or a refined partition to assess the estimate.
    • Report a numerical solution to a contextual problem with justified precision. Bracket the answer or check the approximation independently.
  • Statistics

    Testing a mean and testing for correlation

    Form hypotheses about a population mean under a Normal model with known or assumed variance. Use the sampling distribution of the mean and distinguish its standard error from the spread of individual observations.

    View objectives (4)
    • Form hypotheses about a population mean under a Normal model with known or assumed variance. Use the sampling distribution of the mean and distinguish its standard error from the spread of individual observations.
    • Choose a one- or two-tailed test, calculate a probability or use a critical region, and express the evidence about the mean in context.
    • Interpret a supplied correlation coefficient as a measure of linear association. Use a supplied p-value or critical value to test for population correlation.
    • Compare a statistical conclusion with plots of the original data. Discuss the effect of an outlier or a mixture of subgroups before interpreting the result.
  • Mechanics

    Motion in a plane and projectiles

    Extend constant-acceleration equations to vector motion in two dimensions. Use components to recover an initial velocity or the position at a common time.

    View objectives (4)
    • Extend constant-acceleration equations to vector motion in two dimensions. Use components to recover an initial velocity or the position at a common time.
    • Differentiate a vector position to obtain velocity and acceleration, or integrate components with initial conditions. Distinguish speed from a velocity component.
    • Model a projectile’s horizontal and vertical motion under uniform gravity. Find time of flight, range, greatest height or a trajectory equation, including launches from a height.
    • Select physically admissible solutions and test the effects of assumptions about air resistance, point size and constant gravity.

Summer

  • Pure

    Differential equations and model checking

    Translate a rate statement into a first-order differential equation for a population, motion or another changing quantity. Explain the sign, units and meaning of each parameter.

    View objectives (3)
    • Translate a rate statement into a first-order differential equation for a population, motion or another changing quantity. Explain the sign, units and meaning of each parameter.
    • Separate variables, integrate and use an initial condition to determine a particular solution. Check for constant solutions that division by a factor could remove.
    • Check a proposed solution by differentiating it and testing the initial conditions. Interpret its long-term behaviour, domain and limitations against the original situation.
  • Statistics

    An enquiry using the large data set

    Pose a fresh question using the board’s large data set and identify an appropriate subset, comparison or explanatory variable. Explain how the data were collected.

    View objectives (4)
    • Pose a fresh question using the board’s large data set and identify an appropriate subset, comparison or explanatory variable. Explain how the data were collected.
    • Use a spreadsheet or statistical software to filter records, chart the data and investigate unusual values. Use a statistical calculator to reproduce relevant summaries or probabilities.
    • Select a distribution or inference method only when its assumptions fit. Report the finding in the units and language of the original question.
    • Repeat the analysis with a different subset or handling of missing data and explain which conclusions are robust to that choice.
  • Mechanics

    Moments and equilibrium of rigid bodies

    Find the moment of a force about a point from its perpendicular distance to the line of action, using newton metres and a consistent sign convention.

    View objectives (3)
    • Find the moment of a force about a point from its perpendicular distance to the line of action, using newton metres and a consistent sign convention.
    • Choose a pivot to remove an unknown force from a moments equation. Combine rotational and translational equilibrium to find loads or support reactions.
    • Model a uniform rod, beam or ladder and locate the line of action of its weight. Test assumptions about contact and friction when judging whether equilibrium is possible.

About this sequence

This is a suggested two-year sequence for standard A-level Mathematics in England. It covers pure mathematics, statistics and mechanics. Check the chosen exam board for assessment and large-data-set details.
