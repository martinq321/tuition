Key Stage 3

Year 7 mathematics

Use number lines, diagrams and symbols to connect arithmetic with early algebra. Explain a result, choose a suitable representation and check whether an answer makes sense.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Place value, decimals and measures

    Read, partition and compare integers of any size and decimals, including numbers with leading or internal zeros.

    View objectives (4)
    • Read, partition and compare integers of any size and decimals, including numbers with leading or internal zeros.
    • Place positive and negative values on scaled number lines and use =, ≠, <, >, ≤ and ≥ to state comparisons.
    • Choose and convert units for lengths, masses, capacities, times and sums of money, including decimal measurements.
    • Round to a chosen place or number of decimal places. Estimate before calculating and judge whether a rounded answer is useful.

  • Signed numbers and calculation

    Calculate changes above and below zero and distinguish a negative value from a subtraction sign.

    View objectives (4)
    • Calculate changes above and below zero and distinguish a negative value from a subtraction sign.
    • Add, subtract, multiply and divide signed integers and decimals, choosing an efficient mental or written method.
    • Evaluate expressions containing several operations, brackets and square powers. Explain why changing a bracket can change the result.
    • Check calculations using an inverse operation, an estimate or a second method. Interpret a calculator result in its original units.

  • Factors, multiples and primes

    List factors in pairs, identify primes and composites, and explain why 1 belongs to neither group.

    View objectives (4)
    • List factors in pairs, identify primes and composites, and explain why 1 belongs to neither group.
    • Find common factors and multiples, including the highest common factor and the lowest common multiple.
    • Write a positive integer greater than 1 as a product of primes, using powers to record repetitions. Compare two factor trees to explain uniqueness up to order.
    • Choose between a common-factor and a common-multiple method for problems about equal groups or recurring events.

  • Algebraic expressions and substitution

    Translate words and diagrams into expressions. Read 3y, ab, a²b and a/b, and write fractional coefficients and brackets clearly.

    View objectives (4)
    • Translate words and diagrams into expressions. Read 3y, ab, a²b and a/b, and write fractional coefficients and brackets clearly.
    • Identify terms, factors, coefficients and variables. Distinguish an expression from an equation or an inequality.
    • Collect like terms and use substitution to check examples of equivalent expressions.
    • Substitute signed values into simple formulae for familiar quantities and include the correct units in the answer.

Spring

  • Fractions, decimals and percentages

    Locate, compare and order proper fractions, improper fractions and mixed numbers, including values below zero.

    View objectives (5)
    • Locate, compare and order proper fractions, improper fractions and mixed numbers, including values below zero.
    • Convert terminating decimals to fractions and back. Explain equivalence using place value or division.
    • Add and subtract fractions using common denominators, and multiply and divide positive fractions and mixed numbers using cancellation and reciprocals.
    • Interpret a fraction or a percentage as an operator on a quantity. Connect one hundredth to 1% and interpret percentages above 100%.
    • Express one quantity as a fraction or percentage of another, including results greater than one whole or 100%.

  • Ratio, sharing and scaling

    Compare quantities in the same units and simplify a ratio by dividing each part by a common factor.

    View objectives (4)
    • Compare quantities in the same units and simplify a ratio by dividing each part by a common factor.
    • Distinguish part-to-part from part-to-whole comparisons and convert between the two descriptions.
    • Split a quantity into two parts from either kind of ratio and write the ratio for a completed split.
    • Use a bar model or ratio table to scale a recipe. Explain when a comparison calls for addition and when it calls for multiplication.

  • Coordinates and sequences

    Plot, label and read ordered pairs in all four quadrants on axes with suitable intervals.

    View objectives (4)
    • Plot, label and read ordered pairs in all four quadrants on axes with suitable intervals.
    • Generate sequences from a rule linking neighbouring terms or from a rule giving the term at a stated position.
    • Describe and extend arithmetic and geometric patterns, and identify examples with neither a fixed difference nor a fixed ratio.
    • Relate a growing diagram to its table of positions and values. Test a conjecture against more than the first few terms.

  • Angles, symmetry and plane shapes

    Measure and draw segments and angles accurately. Mark parallel and perpendicular lines, equal lengths, equal angles and right angles using conventional notation.

    View objectives (4)
    • Measure and draw segments and angles accurately. Mark parallel and perpendicular lines, equal lengths, equal angles and right angles using conventional notation.
    • Find unknown angles around a point, on a straight line and at the intersection of two lines. State the reason for each step.
    • Classify triangles, quadrilaterals and other polygons from their side, angle and symmetry properties. Describe regular polygons and identify lines and orders of symmetry.
    • Label triangle ABC and its angles unambiguously. Explore equal sides and angles by folding, constructing or using dynamic geometry.

Summer

  • Perimeter and area

    Calculate perimeters of triangles, quadrilaterals and compound shapes, deducing missing side lengths where possible.

    View objectives (4)
    • Calculate perimeters of triangles, quadrilaterals and compound shapes, deducing missing side lengths where possible.
    • Derive the areas of a parallelogram and triangle by rearranging or doubling shapes and identify the perpendicular height.
    • Derive the trapezium area formula by pairing two identical trapezia and apply it with consistent units.
    • Decompose a composite region in two ways and check that both methods give the same area. Distinguish linear from square units.

  • Translations, reflections and rotations

    Draw translations, reflections and rotations of shapes on grids and plain paper.

    View objectives (4)
    • Draw translations, reflections and rotations of shapes on grids and plain paper.
    • Describe a translation by its displacement, a reflection by its mirror line and a rotation by its centre, angle and direction.
    • Check corresponding lengths and angles, identify which properties stay fixed and relate these transformations to congruence.
    • Find a transformation from an object and its image, and explain when the information supplied allows more than one description.

  • Chance and experiments

    Use the scale from zero to one to describe impossible, uncertain and certain events, and compare chances in words and numbers.

    View objectives (4)
    • Use the scale from zero to one to describe impossible, uncertain and certain events, and compare chances in words and numbers.
    • Distinguish equal and unequal chances. State the assumption that makes a simple theoretical probability calculation valid.
    • List all outcomes of a single trial and use their probabilities, including missing probabilities or complements, to account for a total of one.
    • Record outcomes of repeated trials, calculate relative frequencies and compare the observations with a prediction about fairness.

  • Data displays and averages

    Pose a question about one variable, decide what to collect and distinguish categorical, discrete and continuous observations.

    View objectives (4)
    • Pose a question about one variable, decide what to collect and distinguish categorical, discrete and continuous observations.
    • Construct and interpret tallies, frequency tables, pictograms, bar charts and pie charts. Explain choices of scale, key and sector size.
    • Display discrete numerical frequencies on a vertical line or bar chart and recover frequencies from a completed diagram.
    • Calculate the mean, median, mode and range. Describe what happens to these measures when an unusual value is added.
