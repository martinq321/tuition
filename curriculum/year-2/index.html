Key Stage 1

Year 2 mathematics

Use tens and ones to calculate with two-digit numbers. Connect sharing with fractions, describe shapes and turns, and use scales, clocks and charts to answer questions.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Place value and comparing numbers

    Read and write numbers to at least 100 in figures and words. Build two-digit numbers and explain the value of each digit, including a zero in the ones place.

    View objectives (4)
    • Read and write numbers to at least 100 in figures and words. Build two-digit numbers and explain the value of each digit, including a zero in the ones place.
    • Split tens and ones in two ways, such as 42 = 40 + 2 = 30 + 12. Use the second split to solve a practical problem.
    • Represent a number with objects, a diagram and a number line. Estimate where it lies between labelled endpoints.
    • Order numbers from 0 to 100 and record comparisons with <, > or =. Count in 10s forwards and backwards from any number.

  • Addition and subtraction within 100

    Recall additions and subtractions with totals up to 20. Use a known fact, such as 7 + 2 = 9, to derive related facts up to 100.

    View objectives (4)
    • Recall additions and subtractions with totals up to 20. Use a known fact, such as 7 + 2 = 9, to derive related facts up to 100.
    • Add or subtract ones, tens and another two-digit number from a two-digit starting number. Use objects, drawings and mental or written steps.
    • Add three single-digit numbers by choosing a helpful pair first. Explain why exchanging the addends preserves a sum and why subtraction depends on their order.
    • Choose a calculation for a quantity or measure problem. Check your answer with the inverse operation and find missing values.

  • Properties of flat and solid shapes

    Sort everyday objects and drawings by a stated property. Count sides on flat shapes and test for symmetry in a vertical mirror line.

    View objectives (3)
    • Sort everyday objects and drawings by a stated property. Count sides on flat shapes and test for symmetry in a vertical mirror line.
    • Describe solid shapes by their faces, edges and vertices. Identify a flat shape on a solid, such as a circular end on a cylinder.
    • Compare two shapes by their properties and explain how a turned shape still belongs to the same group.

Spring

  • Pounds, pence and change

    Label amounts using £ or p and select coins or notes to make a target value.

    View objectives (3)
    • Label amounts using £ or p and select coins or notes to make a target value.
    • Find more than one set of coins for the same amount. Explain why counting the coins alone does not give their value.
    • Add prices or find change in a practical situation, working in pence throughout or pounds throughout.

  • Equal groups and multiplication tables

    Count forwards and backwards along sequences starting at zero in steps of 2, 3 or 5. Use pairs to identify odd and even numbers.

    View objectives (3)
    • Count forwards and backwards along sequences starting at zero in steps of 2, 3 or 5. Use pairs to identify odd and even numbers.
    • Build arrays and connect repeated addition to a multiplication statement with × and =. Turn an array to explain why the factors can change places.
    • Recall products in the 2, 5 and 10 tables and use them to solve an equal-groups story mentally.

  • Division by sharing and grouping

    Model both eight objects shared between two people and eight objects packed in pairs. Explain what the answer counts in each case.

    View objectives (3)
    • Model both eight objects shared between two people and eight objects packed in pairs. Explain what the answer counts in each case.
    • Use an array to connect a multiplication fact to its division facts. Recall and record divisions for the 2, 5 and 10 tables using ÷ and =.
    • Explain why swapping the numbers in a division changes the question. Choose a model or a known fact to solve a new problem.

  • Measuring length and height

    Choose centimetres or metres and a suitable ruler or tape. Estimate the length or height before measuring.

    View objectives (3)
    • Choose centimetres or metres and a suitable ruler or tape. Estimate the length or height before measuring.
    • Measure from zero to the nearest chosen unit, including along a sloping edge. Record the value with its unit.
    • Order measurements taken in the same unit and write comparisons using <, > and =.

  • Mass, capacity and temperature

    Choose grams or kilograms and estimate a mass. Read a balance or scale to the nearest appropriate unit.

    View objectives (3)
    • Choose grams or kilograms and estimate a mass. Read a balance or scale to the nearest appropriate unit.
    • Choose millilitres or litres, estimate a container’s capacity and read a measuring vessel. Compare masses and capacities in matching units using <, > and =.
    • Estimate which of two temperatures is higher, then read a thermometer and record the temperature to the nearest degree Celsius.

Summer

  • Fractions of shapes, lengths and sets

    Split lengths, shapes and collections into equal parts. Find and name a half, a third or a quarter and record it with a fraction.

    View objectives (3)
    • Split lengths, shapes and collections into equal parts. Find and name a half, a third or a quarter and record it with a fraction.
    • Find two quarters and three quarters by counting quarter-sized parts. Keep the whole fixed when comparing the fractions.
    • Show with the same strip or set why two quarters equals one half. Write a fraction calculation for a quantity, such as ¼ of 12 = 3.

  • Clock time and intervals

    Read and write times to the nearest five minutes, including quarter past and quarter to. Draw both hands accurately for a given time.

    View objectives (3)
    • Read and write times to the nearest five minutes, including quarter past and quarter to. Draw both hands accurately for a given time.
    • Use 60 minutes in an hour and 24 hours in a day to describe familiar intervals.
    • Compare and sequence durations, for example by placing a five-minute walk and a quarter-hour break in order of length.

  • Position, directions and repeating patterns

    Describe a straight-line movement from a stated starting point, using left, right, forwards or backwards.

    View objectives (3)
    • Describe a straight-line movement from a stated starting point, using left, right, forwards or backwards.
    • Use clockwise and anticlockwise to give quarter-, half- and three-quarter-turn instructions. Link each quarter-turn to one right angle.
    • Arrange shapes or objects in a repeating sequence, including shapes that turn. Explain the rule and predict a missing term.

  • Collecting and comparing data

    Choose a question with distinct categories, collect responses in a tally chart and record the totals in a table.

    View objectives (3)
    • Choose a question with distinct categories, collect responses in a tally chart and record the totals in a table.
    • Create and read a block diagram and a simple pictogram. Explain what one block or symbol represents.
    • Order the categories by frequency. Ask a question about the total and one about a difference, then use the data to answer both.
