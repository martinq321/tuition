GCSE Mathematics

Year 11 mathematics

The second GCSE year connects earlier methods through modelling, geometric reasoning, data and probability. Foundation and Higher pupils share the core units. Each unit identifies its additional Higher content.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Direct and inverse proportion, growth and decay

    Compare a direct proportion with an inverse proportion using tables, graphs and equations, and identify a relationship that fits neither.

    View objectives (6)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Compare a direct proportion with an inverse proportion using tables, graphs and equations, and identify a relationship that fits neither.
    • Explain why inverse proportionality to a quantity is direct proportionality to its reciprocal. Use a given proportional equation to calculate a missing quantity.
    • Distinguish simple interest from compound interest. Set up repeated multipliers for savings, depreciation and population change and interpret a result.
    • Read a conversion, tariff or reciprocal graph to estimate an unknown and interpret both the units and the realistic domain.

    Higher only

    • Construct proportionality equations from a stated relationship, including a power or root, and calibrate the constant using known values.
    • Model a repeated process with a recurrence, interpret exponential graphs in context and explain when the model ceases to be plausible.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Simultaneous equations and functions

    Form two equations for two unknowns from prices, mixtures or geometric information. State what each variable measures.

    View objectives (6)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Form two equations for two unknowns from prices, mixtures or geometric information. State what each variable measures.
    • Solve a linear pair by elimination or substitution. Interpret and check both coordinates, or identify when the lines are parallel or coincide.
    • Estimate a solution by intersecting two graphs and compare the precision of a plotted result with an algebraic answer.
    • Use a simple input–output rule forwards and work backwards for individual numerical inputs.

    Higher only

    • Express an inverse function algebraically where it is defined. Compose two functions in the specified order and check their outputs.
    • Solve a linear and a quadratic equation simultaneously using substitution, and identify graphically when there are zero, one or two intersections.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Combined events and conditional probability

    Represent two-stage experiments with tree diagrams, Venn diagrams, sample-space grids or two-way tables. Explain why the branch probabilities from a node sum to one.

    View objectives (4)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Represent two-stage experiments with tree diagrams, Venn diagrams, sample-space grids or two-way tables. Explain why the branch probabilities from a node sum to one.
    • Multiply along a path and add mutually exclusive paths to calculate a combined probability. Check the answer against a complementary event.
    • Distinguish independence from mutual exclusivity. Update probabilities when sampling without replacement and state the assumptions when using the same probabilities again.

    Higher only

    • Calculate and interpret the probability of one event given another using tables, trees or Venn diagrams. Use expected frequencies to compare the original and restricted sample spaces.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Quadratic equations, curves and inequalities

    Relate the factors of a monic quadratic to its roots, locate intercepts and identify the turning point on its graph. Use a graph for an approximate root when factorising is unsuitable.

    View objectives (7)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Relate the factors of a monic quadratic to its roots, locate intercepts and identify the turning point on its graph. Use a graph for an approximate root when factorising is unsuitable.
    • Sketch or recognise straight, quadratic, simple cubic and reciprocal graphs. State why the graph of y = 1/x has no point with x = 0.

    Higher only

    • Expand products of three or more binomials and factorise non-monic quadratic expressions. Rearrange a quadratic equation into a useful form.
    • Complete the square to locate a turning point. Select factorisation, completing the square or the quadratic formula to find exact or approximate roots.
    • Solve a quadratic inequality using critical values and a sign diagram or graph. Give the solution in set notation.
    • Plot the feasible region for linear inequalities in two variables and distinguish included from excluded boundaries.
    • Sketch translations and reflections of a given graph, including changes made inside and outside the function.

Spring

  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Geometric proof, circle theorems and coordinates

    Write a chain of reasons establishing an angle or side relationship using congruence, similarity, parallel lines or special quadrilaterals.

    View objectives (5)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Write a chain of reasons establishing an angle or side relationship using congruence, similarity, parallel lines or special quadrilaterals.
    • Use coordinates, gradients and Pythagoras to test geometric claims and check whether a drawing satisfies its stated conditions.

    Higher only

    • Prove and apply circle results for central and inscribed angles standing on the same arc, angles in a semicircle or the same segment, and opposite angles of cyclic quadrilaterals.
    • Prove and use radius–tangent perpendicularity, equal tangents from an external point, the perpendicular from a centre to a chord, and the alternate segment theorem.
    • Use x² + y² = r² for a circle centred at the origin. Find the tangent at a point using its radius, including horizontal and vertical special cases.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Triangle calculations and three-dimensional geometry

    Select Pythagoras or a trigonometric ratio in a two-dimensional multi-step problem. Mark an auxiliary line and keep exact intermediate lengths where appropriate.

    View objectives (5)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Select Pythagoras or a trigonometric ratio in a two-dimensional multi-step problem. Mark an auxiliary line and keep exact intermediate lengths where appropriate.
    • Combine a plan or elevation with lengths, surface areas or volumes to recover a missing dimension and give the result in sensible units.

    Higher only

    • Identify right triangles in three-dimensional figures to find a space diagonal or angle between a line and a plane.
    • Select the sine or cosine rule for a non-right triangle. Check whether the information permits two triangles when using the sine rule.
    • Use half the product of two sides and the sine of their included angle to find an area or to recover a missing length or angle.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Comparing distributions and judging evidence

    Compare two samples or time series using appropriate charts, summary statistics, units and the context. Explain the limitations of the sampling method.

    View objectives (5)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Compare two samples or time series using appropriate charts, summary statistics, units and the context. Explain the limitations of the sampling method.
    • Assess a claim based on a chart or scatter plot by considering the scale, sample size, outliers and possible extrapolation.

    Higher only

    • Construct and read histograms for grouped discrete or continuous data, with equal or unequal class widths. Use area and frequency density to recover missing frequencies.
    • Plot cumulative frequency against class boundaries and estimate medians, quartiles, proportions and interquartile ranges.
    • Construct and compare box plots, explaining what differences in the median and interquartile range suggest about the groups.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Exact forms, algebraic manipulation and proof

    Maintain exact fractions or multiples of π through a multistep calculation. Compare the exact answer with an appropriately rounded estimate.

    View objectives (5)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Maintain exact fractions or multiples of π through a multistep calculation. Compare the exact answer with an appropriately rounded estimate.
    • Use a valid algebraic argument to show that two expressions are equivalent, or give a counterexample to challenge a general claim.

    Higher only

    • Simplify surds by extracting square factors, combine like surds, and rationalise a surd denominator.
    • Manipulate algebraic fractions by factorising and using common denominators, recording excluded values.
    • Construct algebraic proofs about integers or identities. Use scalar multiples and vector routes to prove geometric results such as collinearity or parallelism.

Summer

  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Graphs, motion and rates of change

    Plot distance–time, speed–time and unfamiliar financial graphs from tables or descriptions. Read values from piecewise graphs and estimate when two models cross.

    View objectives (7)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Plot distance–time, speed–time and unfamiliar financial graphs from tables or descriptions. Read values from piecewise graphs and estimate when two models cross.
    • Use a straight-line gradient to calculate a rate, with units. Explain how a horizontal segment has different meanings on distance–time and speed–time graphs.
    • Compare constant accelerations using slopes on straight sections of speed–time graphs. Plot a reciprocal relationship, such as journey time against speed for a fixed distance, and interpret its shape.

    Higher only

    • Estimate an average rate using a chord and an instantaneous rate using a tangent. Interpret gradients in motion and financial models.
    • Estimate area under a non-linear graph using strips and interpret the units, including displacement from a velocity–time graph.
    • Plot an exponential model from a table or equation, such as the spread of a culture growing by a fixed factor, and interpret what the graph predicts. Recognise exponential curves with positive base.
    • Sketch sine, cosine and tangent for angles beyond one turn in degrees, including the asymptotes of the tangent graph.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Numerical solutions and connected modelling

    Choose an equation, diagram or graph for an unfamiliar problem. State assumptions, select a method, then check that the scale, units and precision of the answer make sense.

    View objectives (4)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Choose an equation, diagram or graph for an unfamiliar problem. State assumptions, select a method, then check that the scale, units and precision of the answer make sense.
    • Compare approximate and exact answers in a problem linking measurement, finance or geometry. Explain how a changed assumption would affect the answer.

    Higher only

    • Apply a supplied iteration formula with suffix notation to approximate a solution. Check whether successive values settle and verify the result in the original equation.
    • Combine an exact model with measurement bounds to determine whether a conclusion is guaranteed or depends on the accuracy of the inputs.

About this sequence

This is a suggested two-year GCSE sequence for England. Foundation and Higher share core content. Higher-only objectives are identified within each unit.
