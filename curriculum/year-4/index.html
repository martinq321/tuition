Lower Key Stage 2

Year 4 mathematics

Work confidently beyond 1,000, link fractions to decimals and use the multiplication tables through 12 × 12. Measure, draw and compare shapes, and interpret what graphs say about change.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Place value

    Build and partition four-digit numbers in more than one way. Read the value of a digit even when another place contains zero.

    View objectives (6)
    • Build and partition four-digit numbers in more than one way. Read the value of a digit even when another place contains zero.
    • Place numbers beyond 1,000 on a partly labelled number line, estimate a missing position and justify an ordering.
    • Count in 25s and 1,000s. Find the number 1,000 before or after a given number, including across a thousands boundary.
    • Count backwards through zero and interpret the negative values reached.
    • Round to the nearest 10, 100 or 1,000. Explain what to do at the midpoint and when an estimate is useful in a practical problem.
    • Read Roman numerals from I to C. Compare how Roman numerals and the later place-value system record numbers, including the role of zero.

  • Addition and subtraction

    Add and subtract numbers of up to four digits, choosing mental calculation or a column layout.

    View objectives (4)
    • Add and subtract numbers of up to four digits, choosing mental calculation or a column layout.
    • Explain each exchange, including subtraction across a zero. Keep digits of equal value aligned.
    • Estimate an answer and then check the exact result by reversing the calculation.
    • Choose and justify both steps in a problem involving addition or subtraction, such as comparing two combined distances.

  • Area

    Describe area as the amount of surface a shape covers. Use equal square units without gaps or overlaps.

    View objectives (3)
    • Describe area as the amount of surface a shape covers. Use equal square units without gaps or overlaps.
    • Find the area of a rectilinear shape by counting squares, including shapes that can be split into rows or rectangles.
    • Draw two different rectilinear shapes with the same area and explain how the grid proves they match.

  • Multiplication and division A

    Count in 6s, 7s and 9s. Link these sequences to multiplication facts and their related divisions.

    View objectives (4)
    • Count in 6s, 7s and 9s. Link these sequences to multiplication facts and their related divisions.
    • Recall products and division facts for all tables through 12 × 12, including when a factor is missing.
    • Explain the effects of multiplying by zero or one and of dividing by one. Use a known fact to work out a related fact mentally.
    • Find factor pairs. Reorder factors or regroup three factors to make a calculation easier.

Spring

  • Multiplication and division B

    Multiply a two- or three-digit number by one digit in a written layout. Explain the value carried from each column.

    View objectives (4)
    • Multiply a two- or three-digit number by one digit in a written layout. Explain the value carried from each column.
    • Split a factor into tens and ones, then add the partial products. Show why this gives the same result as a written multiplication.
    • Use known facts and place value to multiply or divide mentally, including cases such as 720 ÷ 9.
    • Choose calculations for integer scaling, multiplication followed by addition, and correspondence problems such as matching any of four tops with any of three skirts.

  • Length and perimeter

    Choose metres or centimetres to measure the boundary of a rectilinear shape, including a square.

    View objectives (3)
    • Choose metres or centimetres to measure the boundary of a rectilinear shape, including a square.
    • Work out missing sides using alignments and equal lengths, then add all outer edges to find the perimeter.
    • Convert between metric units, including metres and kilometres. Estimate, compare and calculate lengths, masses and capacities using compatible units.

  • Fractions

    Use strips or number lines with the same whole to show families of equivalent fractions.

    View objectives (4)
    • Use strips or number lines with the same whole to show families of equivalent fractions.
    • Add and subtract fractions with a common denominator. Explain why the size of each part stays fixed.
    • Find unit and non-unit fractions of quantities with whole-number answers, using division to find one part and multiplication for several parts.
    • Solve division and sharing problems using a fraction, explaining which quantity represents the whole.

  • Decimals A

    Divide a whole into 100 equal parts and a tenth into 10 equal parts. Show why each small part is one hundredth.

    View objectives (3)
    • Divide a whole into 100 equal parts and a tenth into 10 equal parts. Show why each small part is one hundredth.
    • Count forwards and backwards in hundredths, crossing a tenth or a whole. Match tenths and hundredths to fraction and decimal notation.
    • Divide one- and two-digit whole numbers by 10 and 100. Identify what each resulting digit is worth in ones, tenths or hundredths.

Summer

  • Decimals B

    Match one quarter, one half and three quarters to 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 using a diagram or a number line.

    View objectives (4)
    • Match one quarter, one half and three quarters to 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 using a diagram or a number line.
    • Order decimals with the same number of decimal places, using examples up to two places.
    • Round a number with one decimal place to the nearest whole number, including a value halfway between two whole numbers.
    • Solve simple measurement problems with fractions and decimals to two places, explaining the unit used.

  • Money

    Represent the same price in pounds and pence and record amounts such as eight pence as £0.08.

    View objectives (3)
    • Represent the same price in pounds and pence and record amounts such as eight pence as £0.08.
    • Estimate a total, work out the exact bill or change and compare two amounts using both pounds and pence.
    • Solve a two-step money problem that includes a fraction or decimal amount.

  • Time

    Read analogue and digital clocks. Record the same time in 12-hour and 24-hour notation.

    View objectives (3)
    • Read analogue and digital clocks. Record the same time in 12-hour and 24-hour notation.
    • Convert hours to minutes and minutes to seconds, and use these relationships to calculate durations.
    • Solve calendar problems by relating weeks to days and years to months.

  • Shape and symmetry

    Sort and compare triangles and quadrilaterals by their sides, angles and sizes, including examples turned into unfamiliar positions.

    View objectives (4)
    • Sort and compare triangles and quadrilaterals by their sides, angles and sizes, including examples turned into unfamiliar positions.
    • Identify acute and obtuse angles. Order angles up to a straight angle, using a right angle as a reference.
    • Find each line of symmetry in a 2-D shape, including diagonal lines when the shape is rotated.
    • Complete a figure on the other side of a given mirror line and check corresponding distances.

  • Statistics

    Distinguish a count from a measurement that varies continuously. Choose a bar chart or a time graph to display the data.

    View objectives (3)
    • Distinguish a count from a measurement that varies continuously. Choose a bar chart or a time graph to display the data.
    • Give a graph an appropriate scale, label its axes and read intermediate values.
    • Compare values, calculate totals and find differences using bar charts, pictograms, tables and graphs.

  • Position and direction

    Read and plot ordered coordinates in the first quadrant, moving across before moving up.

    View objectives (3)
    • Read and plot ordered coordinates in the first quadrant, moving across before moving up.
    • Plot given vertices and join them in the correct order to complete a polygon.
    • Describe a translation in horizontal and vertical grid units. Predict the new position before drawing it.
