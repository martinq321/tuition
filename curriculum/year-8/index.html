Key Stage 3

Year 8 mathematics

Develop proportional, algebraic and geometric reasoning. Use equations, graphs and constructions to show why a pattern or relationship holds.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Powers, roots and standard form

    Recognise common powers of 2, 3, 4 and 5. Interpret positive, zero and negative integer powers of a non-zero base.

    View objectives (4)
    • Recognise common powers of 2, 3, 4 and 5. Interpret positive, zero and negative integer powers of a non-zero base.
    • Use square, cube and higher real roots as inverses of powers where defined. Distinguish the principal square root from the two solutions of x² = 9.
    • Distinguish an exact root from a rounded decimal. Use a calculator to approximate roots and check their size against nearby powers.
    • Rewrite large and small numbers in standard form a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10, including n = 0, and order numbers given in different forms.

  • Signed fractions and mixed calculations

    Choose a common denominator or a decimal equivalent to compare signed fractional quantities.

    View objectives (4)
    • Choose a common denominator or a decimal equivalent to compare signed fractional quantities.
    • Use the four operations with signed proper fractions, improper fractions and mixed numbers, showing written steps when needed.
    • Evaluate mixed calculations involving brackets, powers, roots and reciprocals. Distinguish (−3)² from −3².
    • Choose a fraction, a decimal or a percentage to simplify a calculation and justify the accuracy of the result.

  • Equivalence and linear equations

    Use the distributive law to expand a single term over a bracket, and reverse the process by taking out a common factor.

    View objectives (4)
    • Use the distributive law to expand a single term over a bracket, and reverse the process by taking out a common factor.
    • Transform linear equations using the same operation on both sides. Solve equations with brackets, fractional coefficients and unknowns on both sides.
    • Represent an unfamiliar problem by choosing an unknown and forming an equation; check the solution in the original context.
    • Distinguish an identity from an equation true for particular values. Use a counterexample to reject an incorrect simplification.

  • Linear sequences and straight-line graphs

    Connect the common difference of an arithmetic sequence to its position-to-term rule and derive its nth term.

    View objectives (4)
    • Connect the common difference of an arithmetic sequence to its position-to-term rule and derive its nth term.
    • Translate a linear rule into a table of values and a graph on scaled axes, including horizontal and vertical lines.
    • Calculate a non-vertical line’s gradient from coordinate changes and identify its vertical intercept from its graph, equation or table.
    • Rearrange a non-vertical linear equation in two variables into y = mx + c. Interpret m and c for a fixed fee plus a charge per unit.

Spring

  • Percentage change and finance

    Represent an increase or decrease by a single multiplier and calculate percentage change relative to the original quantity.

    View objectives (4)
    • Represent an increase or decrease by a single multiplier and calculate percentage change relative to the original quantity.
    • Find an original amount after a stated percentage increase or reduction and check it by working forwards.
    • Compare discounts and price changes using a common baseline. Explain why equal percentage increases and decreases do not cancel.
    • Model simple interest on an unchanged principal, distinguish the interest from the final balance and interpret the time units.

  • Parallel lines and polygon angles

    Use alternate and corresponding angles at parallel lines and explain why parallelism is needed.

    View objectives (4)
    • Use alternate and corresponding angles at parallel lines and explain why parallelism is needed.
    • Use parallel lines to establish the triangle angle sum. Deduce the angle sum for an n-sided simple convex polygon by triangulation.
    • Calculate interior and exterior angles of regular polygons, justify the full turn of exterior angles and work backwards to find the number of sides.
    • Combine angle facts and properties of quadrilaterals in short deductions, distinguishing given information from the appearance of a diagram.

  • Constructions and congruence

    Construct a perpendicular bisector and an angle bisector with ruler and compass, explaining how equal radii justify the construction.

    View objectives (4)
    • Construct a perpendicular bisector and an angle bisector with ruler and compass, explaining how equal radii justify the construction.
    • Construct a perpendicular at a point on a line and from a point outside it. Use it to measure the shortest distance from a point to the line.
    • Draw triangles accurately from specified lengths and angles, identify corresponding sides and angles and test whether the data determine a unique triangle.
    • Use SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS to justify congruence. Explain why three equal pairs of angles can still give triangles of different sizes.

  • Scale drawings, maps and enlargement

    Read and produce scale drawings and maps, converting between lengths in the drawing and in the world.

    View objectives (4)
    • Read and produce scale drawings and maps, converting between lengths in the drawing and in the world.
    • Draw enlargements from a centre using positive scale factors, including reductions, with and without a coordinate grid.
    • Identify similar shapes from corresponding angles and proportional sides. Find a missing length using a scale factor.
    • Compare similarity and congruence, using corresponding sides to write ratios in consistent order.

Summer

  • Rates and direct proportion

    Find unit prices and compare best buys after converting quantities to a common unit, including quantities below one unit.

    View objectives (4)
    • Find unit prices and compare best buys after converting quantities to a common unit, including quantities below one unit.
    • Relate speed, distance and time and convert compound units when comparing journeys.
    • Build direct-proportion models from ratio tables. Explain why the ratio stays fixed and identify it as the gradient in y = kx.
    • Recognise a straight-line graph through the origin as a direct-proportion relationship, and explain a counterexample with a fixed starting charge.

  • Solids, prisms and volume

    Describe and compare cubes, cuboids, prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones and spheres through their faces, curved surfaces, edges and vertices where applicable.

    View objectives (4)
    • Describe and compare cubes, cuboids, prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones and spheres through their faces, curved surfaces, edges and vertices where applicable.
    • Derive the volume of a cuboid from layers of cubes and generalise this to a prism with a constant cross-section.
    • Calculate volumes of cuboids and triangular or other polygonal prisms, and recover a missing length or cross-sectional area.
    • Convert area, volume and capacity units by reasoning from the length scale, including the connection between cm³ and millilitres.

  • Sets and combined outcomes

    Enumerate sets systematically in lists, tables, grids and Venn diagrams, distinguishing a union from an intersection.

    View objectives (4)
    • Enumerate sets systematically in lists, tables, grids and Venn diagrams, distinguishing a union from an intersection.
    • Build sample spaces for combined events and record each ordered outcome exactly once.
    • Use equally likely elementary outcomes to calculate event probabilities. Explain why two possible totals need not be equally likely.
    • Identify mutually exclusive events, add their probabilities and avoid double-counting outcomes shared by overlapping sets.

  • Comparing distributions

    Construct grouped frequency tables with clear class boundaries for discrete or continuous measurements.

    View objectives (4)
    • Construct grouped frequency tables with clear class boundaries for discrete or continuous measurements.
    • Choose appropriate charts for grouped and ungrouped numerical data and use comparable axes when displaying two distributions.
    • Choose measures of centre and spread to compare groups, interpreting a difference in typical value or variability in the context.
    • Distinguish exact conclusions supported by raw data from estimates based on grouped data; explain the effect of an outlier or a misleading display.
