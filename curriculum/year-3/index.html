Lower Key Stage 2

Year 3 mathematics

Work with numbers to 1,000 and connect mental calculations with written methods. Use fractions as numbers, measure boundaries and durations, and read charts with scales.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Place value to 1,000

    Read and write numbers to 1,000 in figures and words. Build three-digit numbers and explain how zero holds a place in 405 or 450.

    View objectives (3)
    • Read and write numbers to 1,000 in figures and words. Build three-digit numbers and explain how zero holds a place in 405 or 450.
    • Estimate and compare positions on partly labelled lines. Order numbers by comparing hundreds, tens and ones and use this to solve a practical problem.
    • Count from zero in 50s and 100s. Find 10 or 100 more or less, including across a hundreds boundary.

  • Mental addition and subtraction

    Mentally add and subtract ones, tens or hundreds to and from a three-digit number. Describe what changes at a boundary.

    View objectives (3)
    • Mentally add and subtract ones, tens or hundreds to and from a three-digit number. Describe what changes at a boundary.
    • Use place value or a known number fact to make a calculation easier, for example by bridging to the next hundred.
    • Estimate a result, solve a missing-number problem and check it by undoing the operation.

  • Column addition and subtraction

    Align hundreds, tens and ones when writing an addition or subtraction with numbers of up to three digits.

    View objectives (3)
    • Align hundreds, tens and ones when writing an addition or subtraction with numbers of up to three digits.
    • Exchange ten ones for one ten, or one hundred for ten tens, and show the same exchange in a written calculation.
    • Solve a problem using the column method when appropriate. Estimate first and use the inverse to check the answer.

  • The 3, 4 and 8 times tables

    Count from zero in 4s and 8s. Double groups of two to make fours and double fours to make eights.

    View objectives (3)
    • Count from zero in 4s and 8s. Double groups of two to make fours and double fours to make eights.
    • Use equal groups or an array to build the 3, 4 and 8 tables, then recall products and related division facts.
    • Use a known table fact to find a missing factor or quotient and explain how you checked it.

Spring

  • Multiplication, division and scaling

    Record multiplication and division statements from a story. Use known facts and place value to calculate mentally or find a missing number.

    View objectives (4)
    • Record multiplication and division statements from a story. Use known facts and place value to calculate mentally or find a missing number.
    • Partition a two-digit number to multiply it by one digit. Connect the partial products to a formal written method and check against an estimate.
    • Solve problems in which one quantity is a whole-number multiple of another. Draw a bar or groups to explain the scale factor.
    • List combinations systematically, for example pairing each of three sandwich fillings with each of two breads. Explain why every pairing appears once.

  • Length and perimeter

    Choose metres, centimetres or millimetres and measure accurately with an appropriate tool.

    View objectives (3)
    • Choose metres, centimetres or millimetres and measure accurately with an appropriate tool.
    • Compare lengths and add or subtract them after expressing them in the same unit.
    • Trace the complete boundary of a simple flat shape, measure its sides and add the lengths to find its perimeter.

  • Fractions as numbers and tenths

    Divide a whole into equal parts and locate unit and non-unit fractions with small denominators on a number line.

    View objectives (4)
    • Divide a whole into equal parts and locate unit and non-unit fractions with small denominators on a number line.
    • Make tenths by dividing a whole or a one-digit quantity by ten. Count forwards and backwards in tenths and explain where ten tenths lands.
    • Compare and order unit fractions using the same whole, then compare fractions with a common denominator.
    • Use matching lengths or areas to show equivalent fractions with small denominators and explain how the pictures represent equal amounts.

  • Mass and capacity

    Estimate a mass, choose grams or kilograms and measure it. Read the intervals between numbered marks on a scale.

    View objectives (3)
    • Estimate a mass, choose grams or kilograms and measure it. Read the intervals between numbered marks on a scale.
    • Measure the volume of liquid or the capacity of a container in millilitres or litres and compare two readings.
    • Choose a common unit to add masses or find a difference between two volumes. Give the answer with the unit.

Summer

  • Calculating with fractions

    Share a set into equal groups to find one part, then combine parts to find and write a non-unit fraction, such as ¾ of 16.

    View objectives (3)
    • Share a set into equal groups to find one part, then combine parts to find and write a non-unit fraction, such as ¾ of 16.
    • Add and subtract fractions with a common denominator where the quantities stay within one whole. Explain why the size of each part stays the same.
    • Choose a diagram or number line to solve a fraction problem and check that your answer fits the whole you started with.

  • Pounds, pence and giving change

    Express an amount in pounds and pence and as a total number of pence using £1 = 100p.

    View objectives (3)
    • Express an amount in pounds and pence and as a total number of pence using £1 = 100p.
    • Add prices given in pounds and pence, recording how pence are exchanged for a pound.
    • Find change after a purchase by subtracting or counting on. Check that the cost and the change add to the amount paid.

  • Clocks, calendars and duration

    Estimate and read an analogue clock to the nearest minute, including faces marked I to XII. Write the time and distinguish am, pm, noon and midnight.

    View objectives (4)
    • Estimate and read an analogue clock to the nearest minute, including faces marked I to XII. Write the time and distinguish am, pm, noon and midnight.
    • Match a time of day to its 12-hour and 24-hour readings. Use morning, afternoon or evening to resolve an ambiguous time.
    • Use 60 seconds in a minute and record durations in seconds, minutes or hours. Compare events and work out how much longer one took.
    • Use the number of days in each month and the 365- or 366-day year to answer calendar problems.

  • Angles, lines and shapes

    Find angles at the corners of a shape and describe an angle as a turn. Connect one, two, three and four right angles to turns of different sizes.

    View objectives (3)
    • Find angles at the corners of a shape and describe an angle as a turn. Connect one, two, three and four right angles to turns of different sizes.
    • Compare an angle with a right angle, including when the angle is tilted. Identify horizontal and vertical lines and pairs of parallel or perpendicular lines.
    • Draw flat shapes to a description. Build a solid from modelling materials, turn it and describe the features that identify it from different views.

  • Tables, pictograms and scaled bar charts

    Present the same data in a table, a pictogram and a bar chart. Select a suitable key or scale and label the categories.

    View objectives (3)
    • Present the same data in a table, a pictogram and a bar chart. Select a suitable key or scale and label the categories.
    • Read values where one symbol or one interval stands for several items. Explain how the scale affects a reading.
    • Answer one- and two-step questions, such as finding the difference between one category and the combined total of two others. Show which data you used.

About this sequence
