Year 12 · Summer · Statistics

Discrete distributions and binomial tests

Learning objectives

  • Construct a probability table for a finite discrete random variable and use it to calculate event probabilities. Explain what changes for a continuous variable.
  • Recognise the fixed trial count, two outcomes, independence and constant success probability needed for a binomial model. Challenge them in context.
  • Use combinations and calculator distribution functions for exact, cumulative and complementary binomial probabilities, checking integer boundaries.
  • State null and alternative hypotheses about a population proportion, choose a one- or two-tailed test and identify the test statistic. Find critical values, the rejection region and its complementary non-rejection (acceptance) region. Interpret a p-value.
  • Use the sample to decide whether to reject the null hypothesis. Explain why failing to reject it does not establish that it is true. Relate the significance level to false rejection and explain why a discrete test’s actual significance can be smaller.
