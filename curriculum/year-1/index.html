Key Stage 1

Year 1 mathematics

Start with things you can touch and count. Build towards numbers across 100, sharing equally, measuring everyday objects and telling the time.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Counting and comparing to 10

    Match one number word to each object. Rearrange the objects and explain why the total stays the same.

    View objectives (4)
    • Match one number word to each object. Rearrange the objects and explain why the total stays the same.
    • Read and write numerals from 0 to 10, match them to collections and find them on a number line.
    • Find one more or one fewer. Match objects in pairs to decide which collection has more, fewer or the same number.
    • Order three collections from least to most and explain how you know.

  • Addition and subtraction within 10

    Split a group of counters into two parts, record different pairs with the same total and find a hidden part.

    View objectives (3)
    • Split a group of counters into two parts, record different pairs with the same total and find a hidden part.
    • Tell a joining or taking-away story from a picture. Write it with +, − and =, and explain what each side of the equals sign represents.
    • Use a known addition to work out a related subtraction. Explore what happens when one part is zero.

  • Flat and solid shapes

    Recognise and name circles, triangles and rectangles, including squares, in a picture or amongst real objects.

    View objectives (3)
    • Recognise and name circles, triangles and rectangles, including squares, in a picture or amongst real objects.
    • Handle cubes, cuboids, pyramids and spheres. Match each solid to an everyday example.
    • Find the same shape after it is turned, describe how you recognised it and sort unfamiliar examples.

Spring

  • Numbers to 20

    Make each teen number with one group of ten and some loose ones. Connect the collection to its numeral and position on a line.

    View objectives (3)
    • Make each teen number with one group of ten and some loose ones. Connect the collection to its numeral and position on a line.
    • Read and write the number words one to twenty and match each word to its numeral.
    • Start at a given number, count in either direction, and explain which of two teen numbers is greater.

  • Addition and subtraction within 20

    Use bonds to 10 to make and remember pairs with totals up to 20. Give a subtraction fact for a pair you know.

    View objectives (3)
    • Use bonds to 10 to make and remember pairs with totals up to 20. Give a subtraction fact for a pair you know.
    • Add or subtract one- and two-digit numbers within 20, including zero. Draw how making ten helps with 8 + 5.
    • Choose an operation for a one-step story. Find a missing number in different positions, for example 14 = □ + 6 or 8 = □ − 4.

  • Tens and ones to 50

    Bundle objects in tens and ones and record the total as a numeral. Compare collections such as 24 and 42.

    View objectives (3)
    • Bundle objects in tens and ones and record the total as a numeral. Compare collections such as 24 and 42.
    • Count on or back across a tens boundary and say which numeral comes just before or after a given number.
    • Place numbers in order on a partly labelled track and explain a missing label.

  • Length and height

    Align two objects at one end to decide which is longer or taller. Order a small collection and describe the differences.

    View objectives (3)
    • Align two objects at one end to decide which is longer or taller. Order a small collection and describe the differences.
    • Measure a length using equal-sized units touching end to end. Record the count with the name of the unit.
    • Use a measurement to choose a suitable object, such as a strip long enough to span a gap.

  • Mass, capacity and volume

    Predict which object is heavier, then compare the two on a balance and record what happened.

    View objectives (3)
    • Predict which object is heavier, then compare the two on a balance and record what happened.
    • Balance an object against equal units to measure its mass. Explain why the same units are needed for a comparison.
    • Compare containers by pouring, describing them as empty, full or partly full. Count equal cupfuls to measure how much each holds.

Summer

  • Equal groups, multiplication and division

    Make pairs and groups of five or ten. Count in 2s, 5s and 10s and match the spoken count to the total number of objects.

    View objectives (3)
    • Make pairs and groups of five or ten. Count in 2s, 5s and 10s and match the spoken count to the total number of objects.
    • With a teacher, arrange equal groups into rows in an array and draw a picture to solve a one-step multiplication story.
    • Solve a one-step division story by sharing objects fairly or making groups of a given size. Explain what the final count tells you.

  • Halves and quarters

    Fold a shape or divide an object into two equal parts. Name one part as a half and explain why an uneven split fails.

    View objectives (3)
    • Fold a shape or divide an object into two equal parts. Name one part as a half and explain why an uneven split fails.
    • Make four equal parts of a shape and name one as a quarter. Find another way to divide the same whole into quarters.
    • Share a collection into two or four equal groups to find half or a quarter of a quantity.

  • Position, movement and turns

    Describe where an object is using words such as above, below, left, right and between.

    View objectives (3)
    • Describe where an object is using words such as above, below, left, right and between.
    • Follow a short route using directions. From a fixed starting position, make a quarter, half, three-quarter or whole turn.
    • Predict which way a toy will face after a turn and give a partner instructions to reproduce it.

  • Numbers to and across 100

    Count from 0, from 1 and from a different starting number. Cross 100 in both directions, for example from 97 and from 103.

    View objectives (3)
    • Count from 0, from 1 and from a different starting number. Cross 100 in both directions, for example from 97 and from 103.
    • Read and write numerals to 100 and show their order with bundles, a picture or a number line.
    • Identify one more and one less, then use equal, fewer, more, most or least to compare quantities.

  • Coins and notes

    Recognise UK coins and notes by denomination and say their values in pence or pounds.

    View objectives (3)
    • Recognise UK coins and notes by denomination and say their values in pence or pounds.
    • Compare two denominations and explain why the largest coin need not have the greatest value.
    • Choose a single coin or note to match a stated amount, such as 20p or £5.

  • Clock time, dates and duration

    Put events in order using before, after, yesterday and tomorrow. Use the days, weeks, months and years on a calendar to describe dates.

    View objectives (3)
    • Put events in order using before, after, yesterday and tomorrow. Use the days, weeks, months and years on a calendar to describe dates.
    • Compare how long two actions take. Time a short activity, begin to record it and choose between seconds, minutes and hours for familiar durations.
    • Read an analogue clock at o’clock and half past. Draw both hands, showing where the hour hand sits at half past.

About this sequence

The programme of study gives primary content by year. Schools may introduce content earlier or later within the key stage. The term order here is a suggested teaching sequence.
