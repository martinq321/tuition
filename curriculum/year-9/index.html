Key Stage 3

Year 9 mathematics

Bring together algebra, proportion, geometry and data to model unfamiliar problems. Distinguish exact answers from estimates, justify deductions and test what a model can predict.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Real numbers, accuracy and error intervals

    Distinguish integers, rational numbers and irrational numbers on the real number line. Show why the sets of integers, rationals and reals are each infinite.

    View objectives (5)
    • Distinguish integers, rational numbers and irrational numbers on the real number line. Show why the sets of integers, rationals and reals are each infinite.
    • Compare exact powers, fractions and roots with decimal approximations, including measurements expressed in standard form.
    • Round to decimal places or significant figures according to a stated purpose and use rounding to estimate a multi-step result.
    • Write the interval of possible original values for a rounded measurement, marking each endpoint correctly under the stated rounding convention.
    • Use simple error intervals to estimate a possible error in a calculated quantity. Keep intermediate calculator values until the final rounding.

  • Algebraic products, formulae and proof

    Expand a product of two binomials, collect like terms, and extend the distributive method to a product of three binomials.

    View objectives (4)
    • Expand a product of two binomials, collect like terms, and extend the distributive method to a product of three binomials.
    • Factorise by taking out a common factor and verify equivalence by re-expanding. Connect multiplication of brackets to an area diagram.
    • Rearrange a formula to isolate a chosen variable, including cases that require collecting terms, and test the rearrangement by substitution.
    • Use scientific formulae with consistent units. Represent an arithmetic generalisation algebraically and give a proof or a counterexample.

  • Linear and quadratic graphs

    Construct and sketch linear and simple quadratic graphs from equations using suitable scales. Identify intercepts, symmetry and turning points where relevant.

    View objectives (4)
    • Construct and sketch linear and simple quadratic graphs from equations using suitable scales. Identify intercepts, symmetry and turning points where relevant.
    • Estimate outputs from a graph and find possible inputs for a given output, including when a quadratic gives two, one or no real inputs.
    • Read the intersection of two straight lines as an approximate solution of simultaneous linear equations. Check the coordinate in both equations.
    • Explain how the gradient and intercept affect a linear model and interpret a graph crossing as a change in which of two options costs less.

  • Direct and inverse proportion

    Distinguish direct proportion, inverse proportion and an additive relationship from tables, graphs and context.

    View objectives (4)
    • Distinguish direct proportion, inverse proportion and an additive relationship from tables, graphs and context.
    • Find the constant in y = kx or y = k/x, then use the model to calculate unknown values and interpret the graph over a meaningful domain.
    • Solve speed, unit-price and density problems by identifying the relevant quotient or product. Rearrange relationships to find a missing quantity.
    • Model a fixed-distance or fixed-work problem. State the assumption that allows an inverse relationship and test a prediction against the context.

Spring

  • Circles, cylinders and composite shapes

    Relate radius, diameter and circumference. Use symmetry and equal radii to justify properties of simple circle diagrams.

    View objectives (4)
    • Relate radius, diameter and circumference. Use symmetry and equal radii to justify properties of simple circle diagrams.
    • Calculate circle circumference and area, leave answers in terms of π when appropriate, and distinguish boundary length from enclosed area.
    • Find perimeters and areas of composite figures, including shapes incorporating semicircles, by identifying the actual boundary and any shared edges.
    • Use cross-sectional area and length to derive cylinder volume, and use dimensions or nets to calculate missing measures in composite solids.

  • Pythagoras and geometric deduction

    Use an area rearrangement or similar triangles to justify a² + b² = c² for a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c.

    View objectives (4)
    • Use an area rearrangement or similar triangles to justify a² + b² = c² for a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse c.
    • Calculate a missing hypotenuse or shorter side, keeping an exact root or rounding appropriately. Check that the longest side is plausible.
    • Use Pythagoras’ theorem to find distances on a coordinate grid and solve problems using more than one right-angled triangle.
    • Combine angle facts, congruence, similarity and quadrilateral properties in short proofs about sides and angles. State the reason for each deduction.

  • Trigonometry in right-angled triangles

    Use similar right-angled triangles to explain why ratios of corresponding side lengths depend on the chosen acute angle.

    View objectives (4)
    • Use similar right-angled triangles to explain why ratios of corresponding side lengths depend on the chosen acute angle.
    • Identify opposite and adjacent sides relative to that angle, and select sine, cosine or tangent to calculate a missing side.
    • Find missing acute angles using inverse trigonometric functions in degree mode.
    • Choose between trigonometry and Pythagoras’ theorem for right-triangle problems, draw a labelled model and check the units and size of an answer.

  • Interpreting graphs and models

    Read and sketch piecewise-linear graphs for journeys or tariffs and interpret a change in slope in context.

    View objectives (4)
    • Read and sketch piecewise-linear graphs for journeys or tariffs and interpret a change in slope in context.
    • Use supplied exponential and reciprocal graphs to estimate unknown inputs or outputs, selecting an appropriate scale and range.
    • Connect repeated multiplication in a geometric sequence to a growth or decay graph, and distinguish it from constant-difference growth.
    • Translate a situation between a description, table, equation and graph. Explain where interpolation is reasonable and where extrapolation depends on an assumption.

Summer

  • Probability models and evidence

    Select a diagram, grid or organised list for a multi-stage experiment and identify which outcomes are mutually exclusive or equally likely.

    View objectives (4)
    • Select a diagram, grid or organised list for a multi-stage experiment and identify which outcomes are mutually exclusive or equally likely.
    • Compare probabilities for sampling with and without replacement by counting equally likely labelled outcomes.
    • Compare theoretical probabilities with observed relative frequencies from repeated trials or a simulation, allowing for variation between samples.
    • Evaluate a claim of fairness, explaining what the evidence supports and how a larger or better-designed trial could help.

  • Bivariate data and statistical enquiry

    Plan an observational or experimental comparison and distinguish measurements on two variables from two unrelated lists of values.

    View objectives (4)
    • Plan an observational or experimental comparison and distinguish measurements on two variables from two unrelated lists of values.
    • Plot paired measurements on a scatter graph and describe direction, strength, unusual observations and any visible non-linear relationship.
    • Use a trend to estimate a value within the observed range. Explain why association alone cannot establish causation.
    • Compare distributions with suitable summaries or diagrams, identify limits of a sample or a grouped display, and state a conclusion in context.

  • Multi-step modelling and decision-making

    Break an unfamiliar problem into smaller steps and name the quantities, assumptions and units before choosing a method.

    View objectives (4)
    • Break an unfamiliar problem into smaller steps and name the quantities, assumptions and units before choosing a method.
    • Compare financial options combining ratio, percentage change, simple interest and a fixed or variable charge.
    • Represent the same decision in a calculation, table and graph, and check that these representations agree.
    • Test how the conclusion changes when a price, measurement or assumption changes. Present a reasoned recommendation with appropriate precision.
