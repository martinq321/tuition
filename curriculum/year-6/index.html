Upper Key Stage 2

Year 6 mathematics

Use the four operations with large whole numbers, fractions and decimals. Introduce ratio and algebra, reason with angle and coordinate relationships, and interpret averages and statistical diagrams.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Place value and integers

    Read, write and order whole numbers up to 10,000,000. Partition a number and explain what each digit is worth.

    View objectives (4)
    • Read, write and order whole numbers up to 10,000,000. Partition a number and explain what each digit is worth.
    • Round whole numbers to a requested place and choose a suitable level of precision for a practical estimate.
    • Interpret signed quantities and calculate an interval across zero, such as a temperature rise from −8°C to 5°C.
    • Use a number line or place-value representation to justify comparisons and solve practical number problems.

  • Four operations

    Select mental, column or mixed methods to add and subtract large numbers. Explain the steps in a multi-step problem.

    View objectives (6)
    • Select mental, column or mixed methods to add and subtract large numbers. Explain the steps in a multi-step problem.
    • Multiply a number of up to four digits by a two-digit whole number using long multiplication.
    • Use long division for a number of up to four digits divided by a two-digit whole number. Use short division when the divisor makes it efficient.
    • Interpret a remainder as a whole number, a fraction or an instruction to round according to the situation.
    • Use common factors, common multiples and primes to reason about calculations. Use operation priority, including brackets, for expressions with mixed operations.
    • Solve contextual problems with all four operations. Estimate the scale of the result, then report it to an appropriate accuracy.

  • Fractions

    Simplify a fraction using a common factor. Use a common multiple to put fractions over the same denominator.

    View objectives (6)
    • Simplify a fraction using a common factor. Use a common multiple to put fractions over the same denominator.
    • Compare and order fractions, including mixed numbers and improper fractions greater than one.
    • Add and subtract fractions with different denominators, including mixed numbers that require regrouping.
    • Multiply two proper fractions, represent what the product means and reduce the result to its simplest form.
    • Divide a proper fraction by a whole number by sharing its value into equal parts.
    • Interpret the fraction bar as division and find a decimal equivalent for a simple fraction, such as seven eighths.

  • Converting measures

    Read and record length, mass, volume and time using suitable standard units.

    View objectives (4)
    • Read and record length, mass, volume and time using suitable standard units.
    • Convert from larger units to smaller units and back. Use decimal notation to three places where appropriate, keeping the base-60 relationships for time.
    • Convert between miles and kilometres using an appropriate equivalence. State the precision of an estimate.
    • Solve problems requiring a calculation before or after a unit conversion and check the size of the answer.

Spring

  • Ratio and proportion

    Describe a multiplicative relationship between two quantities and use integer scaling or division to find a missing value.

    View objectives (4)
    • Describe a multiplicative relationship between two quantities and use integer scaling or division to find a missing value.
    • Share a quantity in unequal parts by counting equal ratio units. Use fractions and multiples to check the shares.
    • Scale a mixture, group or recipe while preserving the relationship between its quantities.
    • Compare corresponding lengths in similar shapes, find a scale factor and use it to find a missing length.

  • Algebra

    Use letters for unknown numbers and write a missing-number problem as an equation.

    View objectives (4)
    • Use letters for unknown numbers and write a missing-number problem as an equation.
    • Substitute values into a simple formula, keeping track of the units in the result.
    • Generate a linear number sequence from a rule and describe the constant change between terms.
    • Find pairs satisfying an equation with two unknowns. State the allowed values and list all possible combinations systematically when the choices are finite.

  • Decimal calculation

    Explain the value of each digit down to thousandths. Scale by 10, 100 and 1,000 in both directions with results to at most three decimal places.

    View objectives (4)
    • Explain the value of each digit down to thousandths. Scale by 10, 100 and 1,000 in both directions with results to at most three decimal places.
    • Multiply a one-digit number carrying up to two decimal places by a whole number, for example 3.24 × 6.
    • Use written division when the quotient has up to two decimal places. Connect the written digits to tenths and hundredths.
    • Round a result to a specified accuracy and justify the rounding in the context of a problem.

  • Fractions, decimals and percentages

    Recall common equivalent fractions, decimals and percentages and choose the most useful representation in a problem.

    View objectives (3)
    • Recall common equivalent fractions, decimals and percentages and choose the most useful representation in a problem.
    • Calculate a percentage of a quantity or measure by combining known parts, including percentages such as 15%.
    • Compare two groups using percentages, allowing for the fact that their totals may differ. Explain which quantity represents 100%.

  • Perimeter, area and volume

    Draw examples showing how equal perimeters can enclose different areas and equal areas can have different perimeters.

    View objectives (4)
    • Draw examples showing how equal perimeters can enclose different areas and equal areas can have different perimeters.
    • Use dissection to connect a parallelogram or triangle to a rectangle. Identify the perpendicular height and calculate its area.
    • Decide when an area or volume formula applies and divide a composite shape into manageable pieces when needed.
    • Estimate, calculate and compare the volumes of cubes and cuboids. Select suitable cubic units, including cm³ and m³, and extend to other scales.

Summer

  • Angles, shape and circles

    Use a ruler and protractor to draw a flat shape from specified lengths and angles.

    View objectives (5)
    • Use a ruler and protractor to draw a flat shape from specified lengths and angles.
    • Classify shapes by properties and size. Find missing angles in triangles, quadrilaterals and regular polygons, explaining the relationship used.
    • Calculate missing angles at a point, along a straight line and in vertically opposite pairs.
    • Label the radius, diameter and circumference of a circle. Use the relationship between diameter and radius to calculate either length.
    • Describe and build simple solids. Make, test and compare nets, predicting which faces or edges will meet.

  • Coordinates and transformations

    Read and plot ordered pairs in all four quadrants, including points on either axis.

    View objectives (4)
    • Read and plot ordered pairs in all four quadrants, including points on either axis.
    • Draw a polygon from coordinates and infer a missing vertex using its defining properties.
    • Translate a shape by specified horizontal and vertical steps, including moves across an axis.
    • Reflect a shape in either coordinate axis and explain how the signs of its coordinates change.

  • Statistical diagrams and the mean

    Construct and interpret line graphs with suitable scales. Use them to answer questions about change and comparisons.

    View objectives (4)
    • Construct and interpret line graphs with suitable scales. Use them to answer questions about change and comparisons.
    • Construct and read pie charts by connecting each frequency to a fraction of the total and an angle at the centre.
    • Solve problems from pie charts or line graphs, including finding an original number from a proportion when the total is known.
    • Find and interpret the arithmetic mean as an equal share. Explain when the mean gives a useful summary in the context of the data.

About this sequence

The programme of study gives primary content by year. Schools may introduce content earlier or later within the key stage. The term order here is a suggested teaching sequence.
