GCSE Mathematics

Year 10 mathematics

The first year of this two-year GCSE route develops calculation, algebra, geometry, proportional reasoning and the interpretation of data. Higher-tier pupils also study the outcomes marked Higher.

AutumnSpringSummer

Autumn

  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Number, factors and exact calculation

    Order signed integers, fractions and decimals on a number line and use inequality symbols to compare them.

    View objectives (9)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Order signed integers, fractions and decimals on a number line and use inequality symbols to compare them.
    • Choose a mental or written method for all four operations with signed numbers, decimals, proper and improper fractions and mixed numbers.
    • Use brackets, reciprocals, powers, roots and inverse operations to evaluate an expression and check an answer.
    • Write an integer greater than one as a product of primes. Explain why its prime factorisation is unique apart from the order of the factors, and use factorisations to find an HCF or LCM.
    • Recognise powers of 2, 3, 4 and 5. Find square, cube and higher roots, and calculate with positive, zero and negative integer indices where defined.
    • Write large and small numbers in standard form, perform calculations with them and interpret a calculator display.
    • Convert between fractions and terminating decimals exactly and explain how a fraction compares with its decimal form.

    Higher only

    • Estimate a positive power or root by bracketing it between known values. Interpret fractional indices as powers and roots.
    • Use place value or algebra to convert a recurring decimal to a fraction, and use division to determine the repeating digits of a fraction.
  • Foundation and Higher

    Algebraic notation, expressions and formulae

    Read and write products, powers, fractional coefficients, quotients and bracketed expressions. Identify terms and factors.

    View objectives (6)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Read and write products, powers, fractional coefficients, quotients and bracketed expressions. Identify terms and factors.
    • Distinguish an expression, an equation, an inequality, an identity and a formula. Use numerical substitution to test a claim or evaluate a scientific formula.
    • Collect like terms, multiply a term across a bracket and take out a common factor. Use index laws to simplify products, quotients and powers.
    • Collect like terms with surd coefficients, for example 3x√2 + 2x√2 = 5x√2.
    • Justify equivalent expressions by rearranging them. Use a counterexample to disprove a claimed identity.
    • Rearrange a formula to make a specified variable the subject and interpret a simple formula as an input–output rule.
  • Foundation and Higher

    Ratio, fractions and percentage change

    Describe one amount as a fraction or percentage of another, including values above one or 100%. Apply fractions and percentages as operators.

    View objectives (5)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Describe one amount as a fraction or percentage of another, including values above one or 100%. Apply fractions and percentages as operators.
    • Simplify a ratio in common units and move between a part-to-part ratio, each part’s fraction of the whole, and a multiplicative comparison.
    • Divide an amount in a given ratio or reconstruct the whole from one part. Check the answer against both the total and the ratio.
    • Test whether ratios are equivalent. Scale a mixture or recipe and compare concentrations and unit prices.
    • Use multipliers to calculate increases and decreases, recover an original amount and solve simple-interest, profit, loss and VAT problems.
  • Foundation and Higher

    Angle reasoning, polygons and congruence

    Draw and label points, lines, planes and angles from written information, and mark equal lengths, parallel lines and perpendicular lines accurately.

    View objectives (5)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Draw and label points, lines, planes and angles from written information, and mark equal lengths, parallel lines and perpendicular lines accurately.
    • Use angles at a point, on a straight line and at crossing lines. Justify a deduction using alternate or corresponding angles on parallel lines.
    • Derive the triangle angle sum, extend it to polygons and determine interior and exterior angles of regular polygons.
    • Classify triangles and quadrilaterals by defining properties. Identify line and rotational symmetry and state what must be true of a square, kite, rhombus, parallelogram or trapezium.
    • Decide whether SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS proves two triangles congruent. Use congruence or angle facts to justify results such as equality of the base angles in an isosceles triangle.

Spring

  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Linear equations, inequalities and straight lines

    Plot points in all four quadrants and draw a straight line from an equation. Find and interpret its gradient and intercept, including in a conversion or cost model.

    View objectives (7)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Plot points in all four quadrants and draw a straight line from an equation. Find and interpret its gradient and intercept, including in a conversion or cost model.
    • Find a line from two points or from a point and gradient. Use slopes to recognise parallel lines, including the separate case of vertical lines.
    • Solve a linear equation with brackets or an unknown on both sides. Check its solution algebraically and estimate a solution graphically.
    • Write an expression or formula from a verbal rule, such as a fixed hire fee plus a charge per hour. Form and solve an equation for a stated total or a geometric constraint, then interpret the answer.
    • Solve a linear inequality in one variable. Show its solution interval with open or closed end points and identify permitted integers.
    • Recognise a line through the origin as a direct proportion and interpret its constant of proportionality as a ratio.

    Higher only

    • Use perpendicular slopes to find the equation of a perpendicular line, including horizontal and vertical cases.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Measures, accuracy, constructions and scale

    Convert between metric units of length, mass, area, volume and capacity. Use appropriate units for money, durations and measurements.

    View objectives (7)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Convert between metric units of length, mass, area, volume and capacity. Use appropriate units for money, durations and measurements.
    • Compare speed, pay and unit price. Convert compound units and calculate with density and pressure, including rearranging the relationship.
    • Round to a stated number of decimal places or significant figures. Estimate to check calculator results and keep sufficient precision until the end.
    • Write a simple error interval for a rounded or truncated measurement. Interpret how precisely a measurement supports a conclusion.
    • Draw and interpret maps and scale drawings. Measure distances, angles and three-figure bearings clockwise from north, and distinguish forward and reverse bearings.
    • Use ruler and compasses to bisect a segment or an angle, and construct a perpendicular at or from a point. Combine loci to describe a feasible region and explain the shortest distance to a line.

    Higher only

    • Choose upper and lower bounds for input measurements to bound a calculated length, area or rate, then decide the accuracy justified by the result.
  • Foundation and Higher

    Sampling, charts and summary statistics

    Distinguish population from sample and primary from secondary data. Identify selection bias and state which population a sample can reasonably describe.

    View objectives (7)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Distinguish population from sample and primary from secondary data. Identify selection bias and state which population a sample can reasonably describe.
    • Use a sample proportion to estimate a count in the population. Explain how the sampling method or the size of the sample affects confidence in the estimate.
    • Choose, draw and interpret frequency tables, bar charts, pictograms, pie charts and vertical line charts according to data type.
    • Read and construct a time-series graph and describe changes over time. Distinguish categorical, discrete and continuous variables.
    • Calculate mean, median, mode and range, including from frequency tables. For grouped data identify a modal class and estimate the mean using class midpoints.
    • Compare two distributions using both a typical value and a measure of spread. Explain how an outlier affects the conclusion.
    • Plot paired observations, describe the direction and strength of correlation and draw a line of best fit by eye. Distinguish interpolation from riskier extrapolation and correlation from causation.
  • Foundation and Higher

    Perimeter, area, surface area and volume

    Find the perimeter and area of compound shapes by decomposing them, using triangle, parallelogram and trapezium area formulae as appropriate.

    View objectives (6)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Find the perimeter and area of compound shapes by decomposing them, using triangle, parallelogram and trapezium area formulae as appropriate.
    • Identify a radius, diameter, chord, arc, tangent, sector and segment. Use circumference and area formulae and give exact answers in terms of π.
    • Calculate an arc length, sector area or missing central angle using the sector’s fraction of a full turn.
    • Describe faces, curved surfaces, edges and vertices of familiar solids. Draw and interpret plans and elevations and decide what dimensions a view determines.
    • Use cross-sectional area to find the volume of a right prism, including cuboids and cylinders. Use faces, curved surfaces and nets to find surface area.
    • Use suitable formulae for cones, spheres and pyramids. Split, combine or subtract solids, including a frustum, to find volume or exposed surface area.

Summer

  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Transformations, similarity and vectors

    Construct and describe a reflection, rotation or translation on a grid, specifying its mirror line, centre and angle, or column vector.

    View objectives (8)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Construct and describe a reflection, rotation or translation on a grid, specifying its mirror line, centre and angle, or column vector.
    • Construct an enlargement from a given centre using a positive integer or fractional scale factor. Recover a scale factor or centre from two images.
    • Distinguish congruence from similarity. Match corresponding sides and angles and use length ratios to find a missing length.
    • Represent a displacement with a column vector or diagram. Add, subtract and multiply vectors by scalars and relate the results to a route.
    • Compare lengths, areas or volumes as ratios in common units and identify which measurements a rigid motion preserves.

    Higher only

    • Construct an enlargement with a negative scale factor and explain where the image lies relative to the centre.
    • Analyse combinations of rotations, reflections and translations and describe their overall effect and invariants.
    • Derive area and volume scale factors from a length factor, and use the relationships in reverse.
  • Foundation and Higher

    Pythagoras and right-angled trigonometry

    Identify the hypotenuse and use Pythagoras’ theorem or its converse to find a length, a diagonal, or whether a triangle is right-angled.

    View objectives (5)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Identify the hypotenuse and use Pythagoras’ theorem or its converse to find a length, a diagonal, or whether a triangle is right-angled.
    • Explain why right triangles with the same acute angle have the same side ratios. Select sine, cosine or tangent for a required length or angle.
    • Find angles using the inverse trigonometric keys in degree mode. Interpret results in diagrams involving bearings, elevation and depression.
    • Give exact sine and cosine values for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, and tangent values for 0°, 30°, 45° and 60°. Check exact values against special triangles.
    • Use an area or similarity argument to explain Pythagoras’ theorem.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Systematic counting and probability models

    List outcomes without omissions or repeats using tables, grids, Venn diagrams or trees. Construct a sample space for one or more simple trials.

    View objectives (5)

    Foundation and Higher

    • List outcomes without omissions or repeats using tables, grids, Venn diagrams or trees. Construct a sample space for one or more simple trials.
    • Distinguish equally likely from unequally likely outcomes. Assign probabilities on the zero-to-one scale and use a complement or an exhaustive set of mutually exclusive outcomes.
    • Record observed outcomes in a table or frequency tree, estimate probabilities from relative frequencies, and calculate an expected count over repeated trials.
    • Explain why, over many independent, unbiased trials under the same conditions, relative frequency tends towards the model probability. Compare small and large samples and allow for chance variation in either.

    Higher only

    • Apply the counting product rule to successive choices and adapt the number of options when repeats are disallowed or restrictions apply.
  • Foundation and Higher · Higher extension

    Sequences and quadratic expressions

    Extend a pattern from a diagram, a term-to-term rule or a position rule. Recognise triangular, square, cube and Fibonacci-type sequences.

    View objectives (6)

    Foundation and Higher

    • Extend a pattern from a diagram, a term-to-term rule or a position rule. Recognise triangular, square, cube and Fibonacci-type sequences.
    • Find and verify the nth term of an arithmetic sequence, including decreasing sequences. Recognise quadratic sequences and geometric sequences with a positive rational common ratio.
    • Expand two linear brackets. Factorise monic quadratic expressions, including a difference of two squares.
    • Sketch a parabola from a table or its factors, use symmetry to locate a turning point, and connect graph intercepts and roots to solutions of an equation.
    • Solve a factorisable monic quadratic using the zero-product property and compare the roots with a graphical estimate.

    Higher only

    • Use second differences to find and verify the nth term of a quadratic sequence. Generate other sequences, including geometric progressions with a surd ratio.

About this sequence

This is a suggested two-year GCSE sequence for England. Foundation and Higher share core content. Higher-only objectives are identified within each unit.
