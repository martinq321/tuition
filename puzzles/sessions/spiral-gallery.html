Deeper maths & educator notes

The first view is a repeated similarity: each frame is scaled and rotated. It is an original drawing, not a reproduction or exact reconstruction of Escher’s lithograph.

The second view uses log z = ln r + iθ. Equal multiplicative changes in radius become equal horizontal steps. Going round once adds 2π to the angle. The negative real axis is the chosen seam, and the centre is excluded.

The third view uses w = exp((a + ib) Log z), so the output radius is exp(a ln r − bθ) and its angle is b ln r + aθ. These maps preserve local angles away from the origin and branch seam when the derivative is nonzero; a coarse drawn grid only approximates the curves. This is an introduction to the mapping ideas, not the specific calibrated Print Gallery map.

Accessible session: make nested paper frames; predict size and rotation; compare screen and paper. Then explore the unwrapping view as a matching game between two representations.

Extension: investigate repeated scale factors, powers, logarithms and branch choices. Ask why the point at the centre cannot be unwrapped.
