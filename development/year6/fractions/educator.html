Parent & tutor guide

From moving pieces to explaining why.

This block teaches Year 6 fraction concepts through models, examples, independent attempts and discussion. Use readiness to choose a starting point.

Open the lessonsCoverage record

A short session

  1. Notice and build. Use Explore, or make the same model with paper strips and counters. Ask what stays the same.
  2. Connect the notation. Reveal one worked step at a time. Let the learner predict the next move.
  3. Try a fresh question. Practice starts with the model tucked away. It is available whenever useful. Record the help used without making help a failure.
  4. Explain and transfer. Discuss the open question, then try an unfamiliar context. Revisit later rather than treating one success as mastery.

There is no timer or enforced route. You can split a lesson across sessions. An adult can scribe an explanation; saying it aloud or demonstrating with materials is equally useful.

What this block covers

Equivalent fractions and common denominators; simplifying; mixed and improper fractions; comparing and ordering; addition and subtraction, including mixed numbers; multiplying proper fractions; dividing a proper fraction by a positive whole number; fraction quantities; fraction-as-division and familiar decimal/percentage equivalents.

This covers the fraction focus of mapped objectives Y6-FR-01 to Y6-FR-05. General decimal place value, scaling, decimal multiplication, written division and rounding (Y6-FR-06/07) belong to later decimal blocks. The fraction/decimal connection here does not replace those sequences. Amount and inverse-amount work consolidate prior learning.

Useful materials

Equal-length paper strips, squared paper, scissors, 24–36 counters or dry beans, a pencil, and a ruler. Keep the same whole visible. For comparison, two strips must represent equal-sized wholes.

All digital models have tap/button or keyboard controls as well as pointer interactions. Text explanations are optional records for adult review, not automatically scored proof.

  3. Ask for an explanation or a changed example without the worked solution.
  4. Revisit a fresh mixed question in a later session. Note hints, materials and adult support separately.
