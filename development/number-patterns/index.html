Why do these patterns happen?

On a number square of width n, moving down adds n. On a multiplication table, each row and column increases by its heading. The same number can appear in several cells.

For remainder colours, numbers have the same colour when they leave the same remainder after division. Try a divisor of 4 on widths 10, 11 and 12. Predict the next row before looking.

A square number is k × k; a cube is k × k × k. A prime is an integer greater than 1 with exactly two positive factors. Here 0 counts as a square and a cube, but not as a prime.

In discovery mode, mark every matching cell, including repeated values. Check gives counts without giving away the missed answers. Reveal is optional.
