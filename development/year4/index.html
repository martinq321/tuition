For parents and educators · teaching notes and sources

Begin with three-digit numbers if needed. Use paper place-value mats and counters labelled 1, 10, 100 and 1,000. Check whether the learner distinguishes a digit from its value and understands the purpose of zero.

Keep practical exploration, reasoning and fluency together. Use a few independent questions after the activity, then revisit after a gap. Offer an extension through explaining all possibilities, not simply larger numbers.

Number patterns

Multiplication facts and square numbers

Start here
Highlight the 4-times table. What repeats?

Investigate and go further

Investigate
Compare multiples of 2, 4 and 8. Which highlighted cells do they share?

Deeper challenge
Explain why every multiple of 8 is also a multiple of 4. Try another pair.

Off screen
Counters and a printed hundred square.

Factor shapes
Challenge & ideasNumber

Multiplication and rectangular arrays

Start here
Arrange 12 counters into a rectangle. Can you make another?

Investigate and go further

Investigate
Find every rectangle for 24. Decide whether turning a rectangle makes a new factor pair.

Deeper challenge
Which numbers make only one rectangle, apart from turning it?

Off screen
24 counters or small paper squares.

36 fences
Challenge & ideasShape and measure

Perimeter of rectangles

Start here
Make a rectangle with a perimeter of 36 units. Count its sides.

Investigate and go further

Investigate
Keep the perimeter fixed and change the rectangle. Is the area always the same?

Deeper challenge
Find the largest area using whole-number sides. Explain how you checked them all.

Off screen
36 matchsticks or a loop of string on squared paper.

Area by counting squares

Investigate and go further

Investigate

Deeper challenge

Painted cubes
Challenge & ideasShape and measure

Counting, grouping and spatial reasoning · enrichment

Start here
Paint a 2 × 2 × 2 cube. Inspect one small cube.

Investigate and go further

Investigate
Try a 3 × 3 × 3 cube. Sort by the number of painted faces; count each group.

Deeper challenge
Can you predict which cubes have no paint before looking inside? No formula needed.

Off screen
Small cubes; colour the outside of a paper cube model.

Investigate

For adults: choosing the right level

These are suggested entry points, not ability labels. A learner may explore the model long before they can analyse it formally. Use the starting question first; the deeper challenge is optional. A completed game is not evidence of curriculum mastery.

Activities marked enrichment connect to the curriculum but may introduce ideas beyond this stage. The questions here are original and independent of White Rose Education.
