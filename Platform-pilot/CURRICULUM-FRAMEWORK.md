# Curriculum framework: all ages and personalised routes

Research checked 16 November 2026. Scope: England. This framework is not a complete course or a claim of curriculum accreditation. The site must identify the jurisdiction rather than treating England's curriculum as a single UK curriculum.

## Evidence and source hierarchy

1. DfE educational programmes, programmes of study and qualification content define reference expectations.
2. DfE primary guidance helps identify conceptual foundations and prerequisites. Its ready-to-progress criteria do not replace the complete curriculum.
3. White Rose schemes supply a reference for sequencing, manageable steps, mathematical discussion and progression. Its public guidance directs users to separate curriculum and ready-to-progress mapping files. Those full mapping files have not yet been imported.
4. Maths Wednesdays adds original investigations, practical companions, accessible entry points and optional depth. These are our proposed activities, not endorsed White Rose resources.
5. Exam-board specifications determine qualification-specific details. These still need explicit mappings for the learner's chosen board and cohort.

## Stage structure

| Route | Reference | Proposed platform use |
|---|---|---|
| Early years / Reception | EYFS and White Rose Reception | Play, physical exploration, observation and dialogue; avoid making formal quiz performance the gateway. |
| Years 1–2 | DfE KS1 and White Rose primary | Foundational number, spatial and measurement experiences; short consolidation with concrete materials. |
| Years 3–6 | DfE KS2, primary guidance and White Rose primary | Connected small steps, reasoning, representations and investigations. |
| Years 7–9 | DfE KS3 and White Rose secondary | Prerequisite-based algebra, proportion, geometry and data pathways; accessible support alongside depth. |
| Years 10–11 | DfE GCSE and relevant specifications | Foundation/Higher requirements with flexible entry and separate enrichment. |
| Post-16 | DfE qualification documents | Separate A-level Maths, Further Maths and Core Maths routes, not one generic KS5 sequence. |
| Resits / adult return | GCSE or Functional Skills content | Age-respectful routes selected by goals and current understanding. |

The annual KS3–KS5 allocations shown in curriculum.json are our draft planning choices. The DfE documents do not prescribe these particular Year 7/8/9 or Year 12/13 topic allocations. Primary year references likewise must not become age-based locks on the learner's account.

White Rose's verified post-16 evaluation concerns GCSE resits and Functional Skills. This review did not establish a complete White Rose A-level scheme; do not imply one exists from the words 'post-16'.

## How Year 4 extends into a coherent system

Keep the same information structure at every stage: a concept, prerequisites, representations, common errors, practice, discussion, practical experience, investigation, evidence and optional extension. Change the mathematical content and teaching approach for the actual stage.

A proposed multiplication route illustrates this: equal groups and arrays → connected multiplication/division facts → factors and distributive reasoning → proportional relationships → algebraic structure. A learner can work at different points in different strands.

For a thematic Curves collection, routes could include tracing and describing shapes, plotting coordinates, comparing graphs, reasoning about functions, and finally calculus or optimisation. A learner can enjoy an early visual investigation without needing its complete mathematical analysis. The brachistochrone is an enrichment problem; it must not be labelled a parabola or assumed to be required GCSE content.

A robust content record should distinguish: accessible starting age, required concepts, intended curriculum objective, full analytical depth, qualification route, evidence needed and mapping confidence. Do not infer coverage from a matching keyword.

## Implementation status

Admin now shows 19 all-age/alternative-route planning cards and the 14-unit Year 4 detailed view. Only the three existing sample investigations are playable. 'Has a sample' means partial relevant support, never full curriculum coverage. The arrays activity supports selected factors/area learning. The curve activity's plotting component connects to coordinates, while its quadratic pattern is enrichment. Painted-cube counting is enrichment and does not establish complete shape/volume coverage.
