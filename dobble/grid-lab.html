A grid that makes cards

Choose a direction and slide a line. Its points become the symbols on a card.

After the last row, counting wraps back to row 0. A direction symbol is an extra symbol, not another square.

Why include a direction symbol?

Parallel lines never meet on the grid. Give all lines of one direction the same extra symbol. They now have exactly one match.

Find out before revealing

  1. Keep two cards with the same direction and different shifts. Turn direction symbols off when making another pair. What changes?
  2. Compare two different directions. How many grid points do they share?
  3. Why might we need a card containing only the direction symbols?
Show the complete construction

The q × q grid has q² points and q + 1 directions. There are q lines per direction, plus one direction-only card: q² + q + 1 cards. Each has q + 1 symbols.

The available sizes 2, 3 and 5 are prime, so arithmetic modulo q is a field. The same integer-remainder recipe does not work for every size: order 4 needs arithmetic in a different finite field.
